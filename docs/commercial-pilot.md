# Commercial pilot brief

This is a testable service offer built around the open-source recovery workflow. It is a commercial hypothesis; the repository has no paying customers, validated price, or measured retention uplift.

## Who to qualify

Proceed only when a B2B support or customer-success team has recurring service-failure escalations, a written concession policy, a named sponsor, searchable operational evidence, and permission to use de-identified case references. Disqualify teams whose current platform already captures the same evidence and outcome measures with less staff effort, or whose data rules prohibit the proposed deployment.

## Paid offer and delivery

Sell a timeboxed, fixed-scope pilot for one support queue or one clearly defined failure category. Agree on the case eligibility rule and success thresholds with the buyer before onboarding.

1. **Baseline and fit:** document the current workflow, current source systems, handling steps, policy owner, permitted data, and baseline outcome definitions. Record existing process measures before introducing the skill.
2. **Configuration:** map one approved concession policy to the sample policy format, validate it with the policy owner, and train reviewers on evidence references, repair criteria, approval records, and privacy rules.
3. **Shadow or limited use:** use the CLI alongside the existing ticket system. Do not change entitlements or automate a customer-facing action. Track eligible cases, data-entry burden, review completeness, and actual follow-up.
4. **Matured review:** wait through the agreed follow-up and repeat-contact observation windows. Review the generated evidence coverage, verified resolution, on-time follow-up, repeat complaints, retention observation coverage, and actual concession cost against the pre-agreed baseline.
5. **Decision:** deliver a written findings report with denominators, missing data, staff burden, risks, and a continue/stop recommendation. Do not attribute retention changes to the workflow without a credible comparison design.

The initial fee covers policy mapping, configuration, training, and the findings review. Quote it after discovery from the agreed scope and delivery effort; test willingness to pay before standardizing a price. Avoid open-ended free pilots that produce no buyer decision or usable outcome data.

## Acceptance and conversion

Before starting, the buyer and delivery owner set thresholds for adoption, evidence completeness, review time, follow-up timeliness, and acceptable data handling. The report supplies numerator/denominator rates for evidence coverage, verified resolution, on-time follow-up, repeat complaints, and observed retention. Compare only cases with the same eligibility rule and matured observation window. Set numeric thresholds from that customer's baseline rather than reusing a generic target.

Convert to a second paid phase only if the pilot meets agreed thresholds and both sides identify a repeatable workflow. That phase can implement a reviewed ticket export or connector, validate the action-time policy check, and set up ongoing operations reporting. A recurring support or integration agreement is appropriate only where the customer needs ongoing maintenance, policy updates, or connector operations.

## Stop conditions

- The incumbent system captures equivalent evidence and outcomes with lower staff effort.
- Staff must rekey most case data or policy exceptions cannot be expressed safely.
- Evidence, approvals, or follow-up observations cannot be verified from source systems.
- Privacy or security requirements cannot be met by the proposed deployment.
- The pilot fails its customer-defined adoption, completeness, or time thresholds.

Stopping after a failed pilot is a valid outcome. It prevents unsupported ROI claims and keeps integration work tied to a demonstrated buyer need.
